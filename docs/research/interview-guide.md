# Teacher interview guide

> **Status: not yet run.** No participants have been recruited, no interview has taken place, and there are no quotes or findings in this repository. This is a question plan only.

**Labels used:** `assumption`, `desk research`, `synthetic`, `self-review`. Every section and question group is a hypothesis about what would be useful to ask.

**Purpose:** test the proto-personas, the job stories and the assumptions log against a teacher's real lesson workflow. The interview is about how the teacher works, not about whether they like a design that does not exist yet. `assumption`

---

## Before the interview

| Item | Note | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Participant | One teacher who runs independent-work lessons; recruited separately, with consent. | `assumption` |
| Length | About 45–60 minutes. | `assumption` |
| Setting | Quiet room, away from pupils; no lesson observation in this guide. | `assumption` |
| Materials | This guide, a notebook or note-taking file, and the one-page concept summary. No prototype yet. | `assumption` |
| Recording | Ask permission before any recording; if refused, take written notes only. | `assumption` |
| Data | No pupil names, no school names, no identifiable pupil work. Use "a pupil" or a synthetic label in notes. | `assumption` |
| Language | Plain language; avoid product jargon and abbreviations. | `assumption` |

**Not a legal or ethics claim:** this is a working checklist, not a compliance document. Any formal consent or data-protection process is handled separately by whoever runs the interview. `self-review`

---

## Structure

| Part | Time | Focus | Label |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1. Welcome and context | 5 min | Purpose, consent, no right or wrong answers. | `assumption` |
| 2. A recent lesson | 15 min | Walk through one real lesson from start to end. | `assumption` |
| 3. Scanning and choosing | 10 min | How the teacher notices who needs help and decides what to do. | `assumption` |
| 4. Projector and privacy | 10 min | What gets shown to the class, and what must never be shown. | `assumption` |
| 5. Occasional use and accessibility | 10 min | First use, training, keyboard, reading at a distance. | `assumption` |
| 6. Wrap-up | 5 min | What we missed; permission to follow up. | `assumption` |

---

## 1. Welcome and context

- Thank the participant; explain that this is about their workflow, not their performance. `assumption`
- Ask permission to take notes; explain how notes will be stored and that no pupil names will be written down. `assumption`
- Ask: "Is there anything you would prefer I avoid asking about?" `assumption`

---

## 2. A recent lesson

Ask the participant to pick a recent maths lesson where pupils worked on their own devices.

1. "Walk me through the lesson from the moment you opened the room. What happened first?" `assumption`
2. "When did pupils start the task? Was everyone there?" `assumption`
3. "What did you do while pupils were working?" `assumption`
4. "How did you know who was stuck, who was done, and how they were working?" `assumption`
5. "Tell me about a moment when you changed what you were going to do next because of what you saw." `assumption`
6. "What happened at the end of the lesson? What did you take away?" `assumption`

**Probes:** "What did you look at?", "What did you do next?", "How long did that take?", "What made that easy or hard?" `assumption`

**Avoid:** asking the participant to generalise before they have described a specific lesson. `self-review`

---

## 3. Scanning and choosing

1. "When several pupils need help at once, how do you choose whom to visit first?" `assumption`
2. "What tells you a pupil is stuck rather than thinking or distracted?" `assumption`
3. "Have you ever had a pupil sit idle for a long time without noticing? What happened?" `assumption`
4. "What would you want to know about a pupil's working before you walk over?" `assumption`
5. "How do you keep track of what to follow up later?" `assumption`
6. "What do you do when a pupil joins late, or when the roster is wrong?" `assumption`

**Probes:** "What would you have needed to see?", "What would have been too much information?" `assumption`

---

## 4. Projector and privacy

1. "When do you put pupil work on the projector?" `assumption`
2. "What do you show, and what do you deliberately avoid showing?" `assumption`
3. "How do you decide whether a pupil's name should be visible to the class?" `assumption`
4. "Have you ever shown more than you intended? What happened?" `assumption`
5. "What should the projector show when you pause the lesson or step away?" `assumption`
6. "If a tool hid names by default, what would you still need in order to discuss the work?" `assumption`

**Probes:** "Would a seat code or initials be enough?", "What would you want on your own laptop but not on the wall?" `assumption`

**Sensitive area:** do not ask for the identity of any pupil or for examples of real pupil work. `self-review`

---

## 5. Occasional use and accessibility

1. "Have you ever covered a class at short notice? What did you need in the first five minutes?" `assumption`
2. "What would you need to see on screen if you had never used this tool before?" `assumption`
3. "How do you usually operate a lesson tool: mouse, keyboard, touch, or a mix?" `assumption`
4. "Do you ever read the projected screen from the back of the room? What is hard to read?" `assumption`
5. "Have you used zoom, a screen reader, or high-contrast settings in your own work?" `assumption`
6. "What would make you give up on a new tool during a lesson?" `assumption`

**Probes:** "What would 'obvious' look like?", "What would you want the tool to say when something goes wrong?" `assumption`

**Accessibility note:** ask about the teacher's own needs only if they raise them; do not infer a disability from their role. `self-review`

---

## 6. Wrap-up

1. "What did I not ask that I should have?" `assumption`
2. "Is there anything in the concept summary that sounds wrong for your classroom?" `assumption`
3. "May I contact you again if a prototype exists?" `assumption`
4. "Would you be willing to try a first-use session later, without training?" `assumption`

## After the interview

| Step | Note | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Write up notes the same day. | Separate what the participant said from what the interviewer inferred. | `assumption` |
| Remove identifiers. | No pupil, class, school or colleague names. | `assumption` |
| Tag notes by job story. | JS-01 to JS-05, plus "new story" if something does not fit. | `assumption` |
| Update the assumptions log. | Only change a status if an actual observation supports it; otherwise add a note. | `assumption` |
| Record contradictions. | Do not smooth over disagreement between participants. | `assumption` |
| Do not report numbers from a tiny sample. | With a handful of interviews, report themes and caveats, not percentages. | `self-review` |

---

## What this guide deliberately does not do

- It does not show a prototype first; there is no prototype yet. `self-review`
- It does not ask the teacher to evaluate a design they have not seen. `self-review`
- It does not collect pupil data or real pupil work. `self-review`
- It does not claim to be a validated research instrument. `self-review`
